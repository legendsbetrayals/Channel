**Title:** Cleopatra’s Final Betrayal

**[0:00 – 0:18] HOOK**

**[Talking Head]**  
(Serious, intense look into the camera)

She was the last great queen of Egypt.  
Rome’s most powerful men fell in love with her.  
One of them gave up the war for her.

Then, at the most important moment of his life…  
her ships turned away.

This is the story of Cleopatra’s final betrayal —  
and the night an ancient kingdom died with her.

**[B-roll]**  
Ultra cinematic: gold light on the Nile, a royal barge, Roman war galleys packed tight at sea, purple sails suddenly breaking from the battle line, Mark Antony watching them leave, a crown falling onto dark water.

**[0:18 – 2:15] THE FULL BACKGROUND – HOW IT ALL BEGAN**

**[B-roll]**  
After Alexander: Greek kings in Egypt. Alexandria’s library and harbor. The Ptolemaic court. Then the shadow of Rome growing across the Mediterranean.

**[Talking Head]**  
To understand Cleopatra, we have to start with Egypt itself.

After Alexander the Great died, his generals divided his empire.  
One of them took Egypt.  
His family became the Ptolemies.

They were Greek rulers on an Egyptian throne.  
For almost three hundred years they held the richest kingdom in the Mediterranean.

**[B-roll]**  
Egyptian temples mixed with Greek palaces. Grain ships leaving Alexandria. Roman senators watching Egypt’s wealth with hungry eyes.

**[Talking Head]**  
But by Cleopatra’s time, Egypt was no longer truly free.

Rome was now the superpower.  
Whoever ruled Egypt needed Rome’s permission to survive.

Cleopatra understood that better than anyone.

---

**[2:15 – 3:40] THE QUEEN WHO NEEDED ROME**

**[B-roll]**  
Young Cleopatra in a palace struggle with her brother. She is driven out, then returns with Julius Caesar. The two of them in Alexandria. Fire in the harbor. A new alliance.

**[Talking Head]**  
Cleopatra was not just a romantic legend.  
She was a politician fighting to keep her throne.

She first allied with Julius Caesar, the most powerful man in Rome.  
With his support, she defeated her rivals and held Egypt.

**[B-roll]**  
Caesar’s assassination. Chaos in Rome. Mark Antony and Octavian standing over a map of the Roman world. Cleopatra waiting in Alexandria.

**[Talking Head]**  
Then Caesar was murdered.

Rome split again.  
On one side, Mark Antony.  
On the other, Octavian — the young heir who would later become Augustus.

Cleopatra had to choose.  
And she chose Antony.

---

**[3:40 – 5:10] THE ALLIANCE THAT LOOKED LIKE LOVE**

**[B-roll]**  
Antony and Cleopatra in Tarsus and Alexandria. Gold, silk, night banquets. Their children. The Donations of Alexandria: eastern lands promised to Cleopatra’s family. Octavian’s face tightening in Rome.

**[Talking Head]**  
Antony was Caesar’s old right hand.  
He controlled the Roman East.  
Cleopatra had money, ships, and grain.

Together they looked unstoppable.

**[B-roll]**  
Roman propaganda: Octavian reading Antony’s will to the crowd. People in Rome being told Antony had become “Egyptian.” Angry faces. War being sold as a defense of Rome.

**[Talking Head]**  
But in Rome, this alliance looked like treason.

Octavian told the Roman people that Antony had abandoned Rome for a foreign queen.  
That he was giving Roman lands to Cleopatra’s children.  
That Egypt was swallowing the Republic.

Whether every charge was fair or not did not matter.  
The story worked.

Rome declared war.  
Not officially on Antony.  
On Cleopatra.

---

**[5:10 – 6:40] THE BATTLE THAT BROKE THEM**

**[B-roll]**  
Actium, 31 BC. Two fleets facing each other off the Greek coast. Heavy Roman ships. Egyptian sails. Wind, oars, fire. Cleopatra’s squadron suddenly turning toward open sea.

**[Talking Head]**  
In 31 BC, the two sides met at Actium.

This was the battle that would decide the Roman world.  
Antony needed a victory.  
Cleopatra’s fleet was a huge part of his strength.

Then, in the middle of the fight, Cleopatra’s ships left the battle line and sailed away.

**[B-roll]**  
Antony seeing the purple sails go. He abandons the fight and follows. His remaining fleet collapsing. Octavian’s men cheering. A war decided in minutes.

**[Talking Head]**  
Antony followed her.

To his enemies, that was the proof.  
The Roman general had chosen the queen over the war.

To later writers, this was Cleopatra’s betrayal.  
She saved her treasure and her ships…  
and left Antony’s cause to die.

Some historians say it was panic.  
Some say it was a planned escape.  
Some say Antony had already lost and she refused to sink with him.

But in the story Rome told, one version won:

Cleopatra ran.  
And Antony ran after her.

---

**[6:40 – 8:00] THE LAST DAYS IN ALEXANDRIA**

**[B-roll]**  
Alexandria under siege. Empty palaces. Antony drinking, broken. Messengers between Cleopatra and Octavian. A locked monument. A false report: “The queen is dead.”

**[Talking Head]**  
A year later, Octavian came to Egypt.

Antony’s power was gone.  
His men were leaving.  
And he began to believe Cleopatra might be making a separate deal with the enemy.

Then came the final cruel twist.

Antony was told that Cleopatra was dead.

**[B-roll]**  
Antony falling on his sword, wounded, not immediately gone. He is carried to Cleopatra. She is still alive. The two of them in a last broken meeting. Heavy silence.

**[Talking Head]**  
It was not true.

But Antony had already wounded himself.  
He was taken to her, still alive, and died in her arms.

If Actium was the public betrayal,  
this was the private one.

A rumor.  
A collapse of trust.  
And a man who could not survive the thought that she was gone…  
or that she had left him.

---

**[8:00 – 8:50] THE END OF A KINGDOM**

**[B-roll]**  
Cleopatra in royal dress for the last time. A quiet sealed room. Traditional image of a basket and an asp, treated as legend rather than graphic detail. Octavian entering Alexandria. Roman standards over Egyptian temples.

**[Talking Head]**  
(Slower, reflective)

Cleopatra did not let Octavian take her to Rome as a prize.

She died in Alexandria.  
Later Roman writers said she used a snake.  
The exact method is still debated.

What is not debated is the result.

Egypt, the last great independent kingdom of Alexander’s old world,  
became a Roman province.

The queen who had used Rome to survive  
was finally swallowed by it.

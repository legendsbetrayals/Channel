**Title:** How a Trusted General Destroyed Alexander’s Empire  
**Estimated Length:** 9 minutes 20 seconds  
**Word Count:** ~1,720 words

---

**[0:00 – 0:18] HOOK**

**[Talking Head]**  
(Serious, intense look into the camera)

Alexander the Great built the largest empire the world had ever seen.  
He never lost a major battle.  
Kings bowed to him.  
Cities were named after him.

Then he died at thirty-two.  
And the men who had fought beside him…  
tore that empire to pieces.

This is the story of how Alexander’s own generals  
destroyed the world he created.

**[B-roll]**  
Ultra cinematic: Alexander on horseback through dust and gold light. Endless marching armies. Then sudden cut to his empty deathbed in Babylon. Generals standing around a map, pulling it apart with their hands. Torches, smoke, cracked marble.

---

**[0:18 – 2:10] THE FULL BACKGROUND – HOW IT ALL BEGAN**

**[B-roll]**  
Maps of small mountainous Macedonia. Philip II drilling soldiers. The Macedonian phalanx moving like a machine. Young Alexander watching, then riding into battle.

**[Talking Head]**  
To understand the betrayal, we have to start before Alexander was a legend.

Macedonia was not the center of the world.  
It was a rough kingdom in northern Greece.  
Most Greek cities looked down on it.

**[B-roll]**  
Philip II uniting Greece. Assassination of Philip. Young Alexander taking the crown. Burning Thebes. Crossing into Asia.

**[Talking Head]**  
Alexander’s father, Philip II, built a deadly army and forced the Greek cities to accept Macedonian power.  
When Philip was assassinated, Alexander was only twenty.

Most people thought the boy-king would fail.  
He did the opposite.

**[B-roll]**  
Epic battles: Granicus, Issus, Gaugamela. Persian banners falling. Darius fleeing. Babylon, Persepolis, the long march into India. Exhausted soldiers at the Hyphasis River.

**[Talking Head]**  
In just over a decade, Alexander crushed the Persian Empire.  
He took Egypt.  
He took Babylon.  
He pushed into India.

He created an empire that stretched from Greece to the edge of the known world.

But one thing he never truly settled…  
was who would rule it after him.

---

**[2:10 – 3:40] THE EMPIRE WITH NO HEIR**

**[B-roll]**  
Alexander in Babylon, 323 BC. Feasting. Fever. Doctors around his bed. Silent generals waiting in a dark hall. A signet ring sliding from his finger.

**[Talking Head]**  
In June 323 BC, Alexander died in Babylon.  
He was thirty-two years old.

Some said it was fever.  
Some whispered poison.  
The truth still is not certain.

What *is* certain is this:  
the empire was suddenly leaderless.

**[B-roll]**  
Roxana pregnant. Arrhidaeus looking confused. Macedonian officers arguing. A map of the empire with no crown on it.

**[Talking Head]**  
Alexander had a half-brother, Arrhidaeus, who was not fit to rule alone.  
His wife Roxana was pregnant.  
The child might be a son…  
or might not.

According to later stories, when Alexander was asked who should inherit the empire, he said only one thing:

“To the strongest.”

Whether he said those exact words or not, that is exactly what happened.

---

**[3:40 – 5:10] THE TRUSTED MAN**

**[B-roll]**  
Perdiccas receiving Alexander’s ring. Generals kneeling, then glancing at each other. Names being written on a list of provinces: Egypt, Babylon, Macedonia, Asia Minor.

**[Talking Head]**  
The most powerful man left in the room was Perdiccas.

He was one of Alexander’s top generals.  
He had the king’s signet ring.  
For a moment, it looked like he would hold the empire together.

**[B-roll]**  
The Partition of Babylon. Officers taking distant satrapies. Ptolemy riding toward Egypt. Antipater remaining in Macedonia. Antigonus in Asia. Tension under polite faces.

**[Talking Head]**  
But Alexander’s other generals did not want one master.

They wanted their own kingdoms.

So they divided the empire into pieces.  
Ptolemy took Egypt.  
Antipater kept Macedonia.  
Others took Babylon, Syria, and Asia Minor.

On paper, they were still serving Alexander’s family.  
In reality, they were already carving up his world.

---

**[5:10 – 6:40] THE FIRST BETRAYAL**

**[B-roll]**  
Alexander’s funeral carriage — a golden, almost divine vehicle — moving across the land. Ptolemy’s men intercepting it. The body taken toward Egypt. Perdiccas exploding with rage.

**[Talking Head]**  
The first open wound came from the body itself.

Alexander’s corpse was a symbol.  
Whoever buried him in their land looked like the true successor.

Perdiccas wanted the body taken to Macedonia.  
Ptolemy stole it.

He took Alexander to Egypt.

**[B-roll]**  
Perdiccas invading Egypt. Failed river crossing. Angry Macedonian officers. Perdiccas being surrounded by his own men.

**[Talking Head]**  
Perdiccas marched against him.  
The campaign was a disaster.  
His own officers turned on him.

The man who had been trusted to protect Alexander’s empire  
was murdered by the men under his command.

That was the signal.  
The empire was no longer one state.  
It was a prize.

---

**[6:40 – 8:00] THE WARS THAT ATE THE EMPIRE**

**[B-roll]**  
Ultra dramatic Diadochi wars: colliding phalanxes, elephants, burning cities, banners of different generals. Maps splitting again and again. Cassander entering Macedonia.

**[Talking Head]**  
What followed were the Wars of the Successors — the Diadochi.

For forty years, Alexander’s former companions fought each other.  
Friend against friend.  
General against general.

**[B-roll]**  
Olympias captured. Roxana and the boy Alexander IV held like prisoners. A dark room. A door closing.

**[Talking Head]**  
The darkest blow came from Cassander.

He was the son of Antipater, the old general Alexander had left in charge of Macedonia.  
Cassander wanted the Macedonian throne for himself.

So he did the one thing that would end Alexander’s bloodline.

He had Alexander’s mother, Olympias, killed.  
Then he had Roxana and the young Alexander IV murdered.

The son of Alexander the Great never ruled.  
He died as a prisoner.

That was not a battlefield defeat.  
That was a betrayal of the house they had all sworn to serve.

---

**[8:00 – 8:50] WHAT WAS LEFT**

**[B-roll]**  
The empire now broken into large kingdoms: Ptolemaic Egypt, Seleucid Asia, Antigonid Macedonia. Three crowns instead of one. Alexander’s tomb in Egypt becoming a tourist prize for later rulers.

**[Talking Head]**  
(Slower, reflective)

By the time the wars calmed, Alexander’s empire no longer existed.

In its place stood several kingdoms ruled by his former generals and their sons.  
Egypt.  
Asia.  
Macedonia.

They used Alexander’s name.  
They stamped his image on coins.  
They claimed to be his heirs.

But the single empire he built was gone.

**[B-roll]**  
A cracked statue of Alexander, half-buried in sand. Wind moving across an empty battlefield.

**[Talking Head]**  
Alexander conquered the world in twelve years.  
His own generals spent the next generation tearing it apart.

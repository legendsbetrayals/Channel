The Sykes-Picot Agreement: in 1916, Britain and France secretly divided the Middle East while Arab fighters were dying for the independence Britain had promised them.

During World War I, Britain encouraged Sharif Hussein of Mecca to lead an Arab revolt against the Ottoman Empire, the uprising later made famous by T. E. Lawrence, "Lawrence of Arabia". In letters, British officials raised Arab hopes of an independent state after the war.

At the same time, diplomats Mark Sykes and François Georges-Picot drew secret lines across the map. France would control Syria and Lebanon, Britain would take Iraq, and Palestine would go under international administration. The Arabs were never told. The deal became public only when Russia's new Bolshevik government published it in 1917. This documentary shows how one secret map shaped the borders, and the conflicts, of the modern Middle East.

Why did Ukraine give up its nuclear weapons? In 1994, the Budapest Memorandum traded the world's third-largest nuclear arsenal for promises from the US, the UK and Russia.

When the Soviet Union collapsed, newly independent Ukraine was left holding roughly 1,900 strategic nuclear warheads. Under heavy pressure from Washington, London and Moscow, it agreed to give every one of them up. In return, the three powers signed written assurances to respect Ukraine's independence and borders and never to use force against it.

Twenty years later, Russia seized Crimea. In 2022 it launched a full invasion. This documentary investigates the deal behind the Budapest Memorandum, why the assurances were never real guarantees, and what Ukraine's nuclear disarmament teaches every nation that trusts a foreign promise.

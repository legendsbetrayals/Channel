DESCRIPTION
The Romanov execution: on the night of July 16–17, 1918, Russia's last royal family was led into a basement in Yekaterinburg. No trial. No mercy.

For more than 300 years, the Romanovs ruled Russia from glittering palaces. Then World War I brought millions of deaths and empty bread lines, and in February 1917 revolution swept Petrograd. Tsar Nicholas II was forced to abdicate. At first, the family hoped for exile. Instead, after the Bolsheviks seized power, they were moved east under tighter and tighter guard to the Ipatiev House.

As anti-Bolshevik White Army forces closed in during the summer of 1918, the family's guards woke them in the night and told them they were being moved for their safety. They were taken to the basement instead. Nicholas, Alexandra, their four daughters, their son Alexei and four loyal servants were shot. If you know the film Anastasia, this is the real story: Grand Duchess Anastasia was 17, and she died in that room with her family. DNA testing on remains found in 1991 and 2007 later confirmed that none of the children survived.

This documentary tells how the Romanovs were betrayed by the revolution that replaced them, and why the new rulers decided the entire family had to disappear.

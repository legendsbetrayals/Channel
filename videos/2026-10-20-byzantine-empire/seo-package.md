The history of the Byzantine Empire ended in 1453 with one man: Constantine XI, the last Roman emperor, who begged Europe for help that never came.

By 1453 the Byzantine Empire was little more than one city. Sultan Mehmed II was building the largest cannons the world had seen. Constantine XI sent desperate appeals west and, in 1452, even agreed to unite his church with Rome in the hope that Catholic Europe would send armies.

Only about 700 Genoese soldiers under Giovanni Giustiniani arrived, joining the Venetians already in the city. A Venetian relief fleet set out too late. On May 29, 1453, the walls fell and the last Roman emperor died fighting in the breach. His body was never identified. This is the story of how more than a thousand years of Roman history ended, and why the help never came.
